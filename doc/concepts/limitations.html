Known limitations

What the app cannot do, or does imperfectly — stated plainly.

Platform

  • Restricted settings (Android 13+, One UI): for an APK installed outside a store, enabling the accessibility service is greyed out until App info › ⋮ › Allow restricted settings. The onboarding explains it; installing with adb install is usually not affected.
  • Samsung aggressively stops background services: the battery optimisation exemption and “Never sleeping apps” are needed (a dedicated onboarding card appears only on Samsung devices).
  • While the accessibility service is off, nothing is blocked (neither apps nor protected screens); only the focus session alert reports it.
  • After an update or a force stop the service can remain bound but receive no events until it is re-enabled (planned robustness work).
  • AppOpsManager.unsafeCheckOpNoThrow is deprecated in API 37 but still needed for minSdk 29.

Friction, not a lock

  • The device administrator does not prevent uninstallation; it adds a step.
  • Safe mode, ADB or a factory reset bypass focus sessions and the trusted person code.
  • Protected screens are recognised by their text; One UI changes may require adjusting the detector.

Zones

  • Detectors depend on the internal interface of Instagram and YouTube; an update can break detection until it is repaired with the diagnostic mode.
  • The mask colour is fixed per app and per phone theme; if the app does not follow the system theme, the rectangle is visible. It may also appear for a split second at the wrong place during a screen transition.

Measurements

  • Monitoring cost (Galaxy A04s, CPU of Mobile Manager while scrolling the Instagram feed for 2 minutes): 6.3 % of one core before optimisation, 2.8 % after (debug build: interpreted code and JNI checks), 1.6 % for a release build. Outside apps with detectors: about 0.6 %. One queryEvents read costs about 40 ms every 30 s.
  • Abrupt power loss: the pause of the foreground app is never recorded; it is closed at the next event, so the time in between can be overestimated. Rare on a real phone, where turning the screen off records the pause.
  • WorkManager refuses a forced run before the end of its period; the snapshot job runs at most once every 24 h.
  • The system whitelist is not exhaustive for every manufacturer; check clock, phone, ongoing call and emergency apps on new devices.
